A Survey of the Landscape

Every existing path has a hole.

Four classes of credential-verification solution exist today. Each closes one gap and opens another. Édiproof is the smallest architecture that closes all four.

№ Solution What it offers Where it fails Édiproof’s answer
I. Manual telephone verification the default at most Indian institutions Direct contact with the registrar’s office — a human voice confirms or denies the credential. Slow, expensive, business-hours only; depends on the goodwill and staffing of the registrar; the institution receives no analytics on how often its degrees are checked. Public, queryable, 24/7 chain — verification in under a second, no telephone, no email.
II. National digi-lockers DigiLocker · EBSI · DXC Centralised online lookup index — a single portal that the verifier may consult. Limited institutional coverage, portal-level downtime, requires bilateral signup; offers a lookup against an external authority, not a cryptographic guarantee against tampering. On-chain keccak256 hash — tampering with any field breaks the digest.
III. Walled-garden blockchain platforms Blockcerts · Accredify On-chain anchoring of credential hashes — a real cryptographic guarantee against forgery. The vendor-supplied UI is the only verification path; the operational guarantee depends on the vendor staying in business; lock-in is structural. Open-source contract — any RPC client, any front-end, no vendor required.
IV. Generic NFT credentials standard ERC-721 experiments Decentralised, on-chain anchoring; no vendor dependency; verifiable by any wallet. Transferable — a degree could be sold by an alumna who no longer needs it. Conceptually incoherent and operationally dangerous. Soulbound _update override — every transfer except mint and burn reverts.

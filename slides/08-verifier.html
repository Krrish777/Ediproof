The Headline Contribution

The Wallet-less Verifier.

An HR coordinator with no wallet, no MetaMask, no crypto experience — opens the page, fills four fields, gets the verdict. That is the whole architecture.

I.
The Page loads.

No wallet check. No MetaMask prompt. The verifier hits /verify and the form is simply there — the WalletGate is deliberately omitted from this route alone.

frontend/src/app/verify/page.tsx · no <WalletGate> wrap
II.
The Ledger listens.

The browser opens a read-only RPC channel to Sepolia via ethers.JsonRpcProvider against a public Alchemy endpoint. No private key is involved. The contract's verifyCertificate(…) is view — it costs nothing to call.

EdiproofCertificate.sol:182–208 · useContract.ts:17–20
III.
The Verdict returns.

The contract recomputes keccak256(abi.encodePacked(name, course, institution, ipfsURI)), looks the digest up in the hashToTokenId map, and returns the tuple (valid, tokenId, ownerAddr, revoked, replacedByTokenId). Four states. One call. No signature.

EdiproofCertificate.sol:399–406 · the four verdict possibilities
From the Editors Why this matters: 90% of credential verifiers will never own a wallet. A verifier path that needs one is operationally dead on arrival.

What the verifier sees

The verifier page — the By Details tab. The four fields produce a live keccak256 hash; submitting against the chain returns a verdict in under a second.
